Introduction

Dr. Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam (1931–2015) was a distinguished Indian aerospace scientist and the 11th President of India. Widely revered as the "Missile Man of India," he played a foundational role in the nation's civilian space program and military missile development.

Early Life and Education

Born into a humble family in Rameswaram, Dr. Kalam learned the value of hard work early in life. To support his family, he sold newspapers, yet he never lost his thirst for knowledge. He pursued physics at St. Joseph's College and later earned a degree in aerospace engineering from the Madras Institute of Technology.

Scientific Legacy

Dr. Kalam's work fundamentally changed India's technological landscape:

  • ISRO & DRDO: He was a key figure at the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO).
  • SLV-III: Served as project director for India's first indigenous Satellite Launch Vehicle.
  • Defense: Led the Integrated Guided Missile Development Program (IGMDP), developing the Agni and Prithvi missiles.

The People's President

Serving as President from 2002 to 2007, he became known as the "People's President." He was particularly loved for his commitment to mentoring students and his vision of transforming India into a developed nation by 2020.

Legacy: Even after his passing in 2015, his books like Wings of Fire and Ignited Minds continue to inspire millions across the globe.
